Example 3.2.5.

Notice that the vectors
\begin{equation*} \vec{e_1} = \threevec{1}{0}{0}, \vec{e_2} = \threevec{0}{1}{0}, \vec{e_3} = \threevec{0}{0}{1} \end{equation*}
form the columns of the \(3\times3\) identity matrix, which implies that this set forms a basis for \(\real^3\text{.}\) More generally, the set of vectors \(\vec{e_1},\vec{e_2},\ldots,\vec{e_m}\) forms a basis for \(\real^m\text{,}\) which we call the standard basis for \(\real^m\text{.}\)
