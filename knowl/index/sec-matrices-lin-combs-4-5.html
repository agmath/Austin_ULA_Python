Definition 2.2.2. Matrix-vector multiplication.

The product of a matrix \(A\) by a vector \(\vec{x}\) will be the linear combination of the columns of \(A\) using the components of \(\vec{x}\) as weights. More specifically, if
\begin{equation*} A=\left[\begin{array}{rrrr} \vec{v_1} \amp \vec{v_2} \amp \ldots \amp \vec{v_n} \end{array}\right],~~~ \vec{x} = \left[\begin{array}{r} c_1 \\ c_2 \\ \vdots \\ c_n \end{array}\right], \end{equation*}
then
\begin{equation*} A\vec{x} = c_1\vec{v_1} + c_2\vec{v_2} + \ldots + c_n\vec{v_n}\text{.} \end{equation*}
If \(A\) is an \(m\times n\) matrix, then \(\vec{x}\) must be an \(n\)-dimensional vector, and the product \(A\vec{x}\) will be an \(m\)-dimensional vector.
